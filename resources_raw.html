The Flash Cell

At the heart of an SSD is the Floating Gate Transistor. It stores data by trapping electrons inside a layer of silicon isolated by oxide barriers.

Think of it as a microscopic cage. Over time, the high voltage required to push electrons through the barriers causes physical degradation. This is known as tunneling stress.

P/E Cycles

Program/Erase cycles are the currency of SSD life. Every time a cell is reset to store new data, it loses a fraction of its structural integrity.

LESSON_02.1

Wear-Leveling Algorithms

dynamic_form

Dynamic Wear-Leveling

The controller selects the block with the lowest erase count when writing new data. However, it ignores "cold data" (data that never changes), leaving those blocks unused and potentially wasting cycles.

schema

Static Wear-Leveling

The "gold standard" for enterprise drives. It actively moves cold data to blocks with high wear, freeing up "fresh" blocks for incoming high-frequency writes. It ensures the entire drive ages at the exact same rate.

CHAPTER_03
LESSON_03.1

Garbage Collection

SSD pages cannot be overwritten directly. They must be erased. But erasures only happen at the Block Level, while writes happen at the Page Level.

01
Analysis Phase

The controller identifies blocks containing 'stale' or 'invalid' pages that have been marked for deletion by the OS TRIM command.

02
Consolidation

Valid data from those blocks is copied to a new, empty block to maintain data integrity before the original block is wiped.
